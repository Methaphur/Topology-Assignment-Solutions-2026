\textbf{Proof.}

Let
\[ A=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_n. \]
Suppose, for contradiction, that $A$ is disconnected. Then
\[ A=U\cup V, \]
where $U,V$ are non-empty, disjoint and open in $A$.

Since each $A_n$ is connected and
\[ A_n=(A_n\cap U)\cup(A_n\cap V), \]
we must have
\[ A_n\subseteq U\quad\text{or}\quad A_n\subseteq V. \]

Without loss of generality, let $A_1\subseteq U$.

Since $A_1\cap A_2\neq\emptyset$, there exists $p\in A_1\cap A_2$. Thus $p\in U$, so
$A_2\not\subseteq V$. Consequently, $A_2\subseteq U$.

Repeating this argument inductively, we obtain
\[ A_n\subseteq U\quad\text{for every }n\in\N. \]

Therefore,
\[ A=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_n\subseteq U, \]
contradicting $V\neq\emptyset$.

Hence,
\[ \boxed{\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_n\text{ is connected.}} \]
\hfill $\square$
