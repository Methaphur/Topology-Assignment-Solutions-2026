\emph{Solution:} Let $x_1,x_2$ be a pair of distinct points in $X$. $X$ being a $T_1$ space,
singletons $\{x_1\}$ and $\{x_2\}$ are closed in $X$. Therefore, $X\setminus\{x_1\}$ and
$X\setminus\{x_2\}$ are open in $X$. Now, $x_2\in X\setminus\{x_1\}$ (because $x_1\neq x_2$) but
$x_2\notin X\setminus\{x_2\}$. Similarly, $x_1\in X\setminus\{x_2\}$ (because $x_1\neq x_2$) but
$x_1\notin X\setminus\{x_1\}$. As $x_1,x_2\in X$ were chosen arbitrarily, we can say that for
each pair $x_1,x_2$ of distinct points, there exist open sets $U_1$ and $U_2$ containing $x_1$
and $x_2$, respectively, such that $x_1\notin U_2$ and $x_2\notin U_1$. \hfill$\square$
