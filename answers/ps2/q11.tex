Let $\B$ be a basis for the topology of $X$.

\begin{proof}
\textbf{(i) $\Rightarrow$ (ii).} We prove the contrapositive. Suppose some neighborhood $U$ of
$x$ satisfies $U\cap A=\emptyset$. Then $A\subseteq X\setminus U$, and $X\setminus U$ is closed
since $U$ is open. As $\cl{A}$ is the smallest closed set containing $A$, we get
$\cl{A}\subseteq X\setminus U$. Since $x\in U$, $x\notin X\setminus U$, hence $x\notin\cl{A}$.

\textbf{(ii) $\Rightarrow$ (iii).} Every basis element $B\in\B$ is open, so a basis element
containing $x$ is a neighborhood of $x$. By (ii) it intersects $A$.

\textbf{(iii) $\Rightarrow$ (i).} Again by contrapositive. Suppose $x\notin\cl{A}$. Then
$U:=X\setminus\cl{A}$ is open (since $\cl{A}$ is closed) and contains $x$. Because $\B$ is a
basis, there is $B\in\B$ with $x\in B\subseteq U$. Then
\[ B\cap A\subseteq U\cap A\subseteq(X\setminus\cl{A})\cap A=\emptyset, \]
since $A\subseteq\cl{A}$. So $B$ is a basis element containing $x$ that does not intersect $A$,
and (iii) fails.

Thus (i) $\Rightarrow$ (ii) $\Rightarrow$ (iii) $\Rightarrow$ (i), and the three statements are
equivalent.
\end{proof}
