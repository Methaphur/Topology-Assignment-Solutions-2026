\begin{proof}
Suppose $\T_1$ and $\T_2$ are comparable. Without loss of generality, let
\[ \T_1\subseteq\T_2. \]
Consider the identity map
\[ I\colon(X,\T_2)\to(X,\T_1),\qquad I(x):=x. \]
Clearly, $I$ is \emph{bijective}.

For all $U\in\T_1$, we have
\[ I^{-1}(U)=U\in\T_2. \]
Hence, $I$ is \emph{continuous}.

Since $I$ is a bijective continuous map from a compact space to a Hausdorff space, $I$ is a
\emph{homeomorphism}. So, $I^{-1}$ is also continuous.

Thus, for all $U\in\T_2$, we have
\[ (I^{-1})^{-1}(U)=I(U)=U\in\T_1. \]
Hence,
\[ \T_2\subseteq\T_1. \]
Therefore,
\[ \boxed{\T_1=\T_2.} \]
Hence, either $\T_1=\T_2$ or they are not comparable.
\end{proof}
