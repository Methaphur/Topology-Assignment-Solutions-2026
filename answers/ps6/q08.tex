\paragraph{(a)}
A quotient map $p\colon X\to Y$ is continuous and surjective. It is a fundamental topological
theorem that the continuous image of a compact space is compact. Since $X$ is compact and
$Y=p(X)$, $Y$ is compact.

\textbf{Conclusion (a):} $Y$ is compact.\hfill$\square$

\paragraph{(b)}
Let $C\subseteq X$ be an arbitrary closed set in $X$. We must prove that $p(C)$ is closed in
$Y$. Because $X$ is compact and $C$ is closed in $X$, $C$ is compact. Consider the restriction
map $p|_C\colon C\to p(C)$, which is continuous. Thus, $p(C)=p|_C(C)$ is the continuous image of
a compact space, so $p(C)$ is compact in $Y$. Since $Y$ is Hausdorff, any compact subspace of a
Hausdorff space is closed. Consequently, $p(C)$ is closed in $Y$.

\textbf{Conclusion (b):} $p$ is a closed map.\hfill$\square$
