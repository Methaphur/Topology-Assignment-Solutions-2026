\begin{proof}
Suppose $g_1,g_2\colon X\to Y$ are two continuous extensions of $f\colon A\to Y$, so
$g_1|_A=f$ and $g_2|_A=f$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
  \item Since $g_1|_A=g_2|_A=f$, the maps $g_1$ and $g_2$ agree on the subset $A\subseteq X$.
  \item $A$ is dense in $X$, and $Y$ is a Hausdorff space.
  \item By \textbf{Problem 3.9(ii)}, if two continuous functions from $X$ into a Hausdorff
        space $Y$ agree on a dense subset $A\subseteq X$, they must agree on all of $X$.
\end{enumerate}
Therefore, $g_1(x)=g_2(x)$ for all $x\in X$, proving that the continuous extension $g$ is
uniquely determined by $f$.
\end{proof}
