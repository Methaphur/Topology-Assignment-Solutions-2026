Let $X$ be infinite and let
\[ \T_f=\{\varnothing\}\cup\{U\subseteq X : X\setminus U \text{ is finite}\} \]
be the finite-complement topology. Suppose $U,V\in\T_f$ are nonempty. By the definition of
$\T_f$, both complements $X\setminus U$ and $X\setminus V$ are finite. Using De Morgan's law,
\[ X\setminus(U\cap V)=(X\setminus U)\cup(X\setminus V). \]
The right-hand side is a union of two finite sets, and is therefore finite. If
$U\cap V=\varnothing$, then the left-hand side equals $X$, which would imply that $X$ is finite.
This contradicts the assumption that $X$ is infinite. Hence
\[ \boxed{U\cap V\neq\varnothing.} \]
