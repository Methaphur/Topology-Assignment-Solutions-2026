\begin{proof}
Let $(X,\tau)$ be a topological space, let $U\subseteq X$ be an open set in $X$, and let
$V\subseteq X$ be a closed set in $X$.

First, recall that for any two subsets $A,B\subseteq X$, the set difference $A\setminus B$ is
given by the identity
\[ A\setminus B=\{x\in X\mid x\in A\text{ and }x\notin B\}=A\cap(X\setminus B). \]
Furthermore, by the definition of a closed set in a topological space, a subset $F\subseteq X$
is closed in $X$ if and only if its complement $X\setminus F$ is open in $X$.

\paragraph{Part I: Proof that $U\setminus V$ is open in $X$.}
Applying the set-difference identity to $U\setminus V$, we have
\[ U\setminus V=U\cap(X\setminus V). \]
Since $V$ is closed in $X$, its complement $X\setminus V$ is open in $X$ by definition. By
hypothesis, $U$ is also open in $X$. By the finite intersection axiom of a topology, the
intersection of two open sets in $X$ is open in $X$. Therefore, $U\setminus V=U\cap(X\setminus V)$
is open in $X$.

\paragraph{Part II: Proof that $V\setminus U$ is closed in $X$.}
We provide both a direct proof from the open-set axioms via De Morgan's laws and a dual proof
via closed-set intersections:

\textbf{Method 1 (Direct proof via De Morgan's Laws).} To show that $V\setminus U$ is closed in
$X$, it suffices by definition to prove that its complement $X\setminus(V\setminus U)$ is open
in $X$. Using $V\setminus U=V\cap(X\setminus U)$ and De Morgan's laws, we compute:
\begin{align*}
  X\setminus(V\setminus U) &= X\setminus\big(V\cap(X\setminus U)\big)\\
                           &= (X\setminus V)\cup\big(X\setminus(X\setminus U)\big)\\
                           &= (X\setminus V)\cup U.
\end{align*}
Since $V$ is closed in $X$, its complement $X\setminus V$ is open in $X$. By hypothesis, $U$ is
also open in $X$. Because a topology is closed under unions, $(X\setminus V)\cup U$ is open in
$X$. Consequently, $X\setminus(V\setminus U)$ is open in $X$, which by definition means that
$V\setminus U$ is closed in $X$.

\textbf{Method 2 (Via intersections of closed sets).} Using the set-difference identity, we
write
\[ V\setminus U=V\cap(X\setminus U). \]
Since $U$ is open in $X$ and $X\setminus(X\setminus U)=U$, the set $X\setminus U$ is closed in
$X$. We are also given that $V$ is closed in $X$. Since the intersection of any two closed sets
in $X$ is closed in $X$, it follows immediately that $V\setminus U=V\cap(X\setminus U)$ is
closed in $X$.

This completes the proof of both statements.
\end{proof}
